## Context & Overview

As the product suite grew across desktop and mobile, disjointed UI components led to brand inconsistency and high engineering overhead. Our goal was to create a unified, multi-brand design system that bridged the gap between product designers and frontend teams.

<Callout type="info" title="Key Challenge">
Over 14 disparate product squads were maintaining their own variations of button, form, and modal components, resulting in 40+ shades of grey and inconsistent keyboard navigation.
</Callout>

## Discovery & Research

We began by conducting an interface inventory across the entire digital ecosystem. This helped quantify technical and visual debt and align leadership on the ROI of a centralized system.

### What We Found
- **Accessibility Gaps**: More than 35% of existing UI elements failed WCAG 2.1 AA contrast requirements.
- **Redundant Tokens**: Hundreds of hardcoded hex values across codebases without semantic naming.
- **Sync Friction**: Designers updated Figma libraries manually without automated parity in code.

<Callout type="tip" title="Design Principle">
"Tokens first, components second." By standardizing semantic design tokens first, we enabled instant theme switching and guaranteed accessibility compliance across platforms.
</Callout>

## The Solution

We structured the design system around three foundational layers:

1. **Global Tokens**: Raw values for colors, font weights, and spacing scales.
2. **Semantic Tokens**: Contextual decisions such as `--color-surface-hover` or `--text-muted`.
3. **Component Specs**: Accessible, responsive component primitives documented directly in Figma and code.

### Measurable Impact

After rolling out the new design system to all 14 squads over two quarters, we tracked significant performance and efficiency gains:

- **45% reduction** in design-to-development handoff cycle time.
- **100% WCAG 2.1 AA compliance** across core journey components.
- **Zero regressions** during dark mode rollout thanks to semantic token architecture.
